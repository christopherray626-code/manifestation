# Win the subliminal market on trust

Your app already has more features than any single subliminal app on either store. What it lacks is the plumbing that decides whether people stay and pay: audio that keeps playing with the screen locked, honest billing, backups, offline listening and widgets. "Sublime: Subliminals", the app that took the name, is a one-month-old, iPhone-only app with a hard paywall, **eight price points from $4.99 a week to $99.99 lifetime and zero ratings** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sublime-subliminals/id6817551957)). It isn't the real threat. The field is about 30 small apps, and only VibeSesh (**1,747 iOS ratings, 10K+ Android installs**) and Decrétalo have real traction among the "make your own" apps ([VibeSesh lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6746336493&country=us), [Decrétalo on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.decretalo.app&hl=en&gl=US)). Across about 890 recent App Store reviews, users' biggest anger isn't about features. It's about **trials that charge without warning, "free" apps that can't be used for free, recordings and streaks that disappear, and audio that stops in the night** ([I am reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=874656917/sortBy=mostRecent/json), [ThinkUp reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=906660772/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). Subliminal fans add one fear of their own: they don't trust what's hidden in a track. That gives you a clear way to win. Ship the reliability and billing basics first, then present the app as the safe, fully transparent subliminal maker. Nobody yet does all of these together: lets you see every word, works fully offline, lets you export your own audio file, and treats you fairly on price.

## Thirty small rivals, no one has it all

The category splits into five kinds of app, and each one is missing what the others have. The **"make your own" apps** (Sublime, VibeSesh, Sublimind, JustSubly, SelfMade, ProgramU, Whispurry, Hopium and others) nearly all launched between March 2025 and October 2026, and most have **fewer than 60 US ratings** ([subliminal app scan, iTunes lookups](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6742783610&country=us)). The **library apps** sell ready-made tracks at the highest prices in the category: Sound & Soulful charges **$179.99 a year** for 130+ tracks, each in 8 background versions, and still earns **4.87 stars from 1,767 ratings** ([Sound & Soulful](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6444500896)). The **affirmation-card giants**, Monkey Taps' I am and Motivation, have **739K and 1.08M iOS ratings** and 10M+ Android installs each, built on ten years of reviews and home-screen widgets ([I am](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id874656917), [Motivation](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id876080126)). The **2026 "dream self" AI apps**, Stella and Aya, went from launch to 3K–5.6K ratings within months by turning AI-written stories into audio ([Stella](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6757347283), [Aya](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6760195623)). And **ReliefMix** is the sound-mixing powerhouse, with 18 voice effects, isochronic programs, widgets, offline play and cross-device backup ([ReliefMix](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1630430321&country=us)).

Sublime itself is a close copy of your basic flow: pick an intention, get OpenAI-written affirmations or write your own, record your voice or use text-to-speech, mix it over white noise, pink noise or "soft ambience", and set speed, layers and a length from 30 minutes to 8 hours ([Sublime site](https://www.sublimesubliminals.com/), [privacy policy](https://www.sublimesubliminals.com/privacy/)). Its strengths are speed (five releases in four weeks), **five languages** in the app and a five-language website built to rank on Google, a free "417 Hz" MP3 offered for your email address, and Superwall price testing ([sitemap](https://www.sublimesubliminals.com/sitemap.xml), [free subliminal page](https://www.sublimesubliminals.com/free-subliminal/)). Its weaknesses are no Android version (the Play link returns 404), no ready-made tracks, no frequencies (its own site tells users to choose sounds "according to comfort rather than claims about special frequencies"), an unnamed TTS voice, and creation locked behind a subscription ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.axiomhouse.sublime), [sleep page](https://www.sublimesubliminals.com/sleep-affirmations/)). It's run by one developer in Alberta under the name "Axiom House", whose earlier app also has zero ratings ([Psyche lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6789704737)).

## Weekly plans and long quizzes, but annual plans keep people

Nearly every rival uses the same money playbook: a long personal quiz, a paywall, lots of price points under constant testing, and often a weekly plan. Subliminal makers charge about **$4.99–$7.99 a week, $9.99–$12.99 a month, $29.99–$59.99 a year and about $79.99 lifetime** (SelfMade: $4.99 / $9.99 / $39.99 / $79.99) ([SelfMade IAPs](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6766178146), [Sublimind IAPs](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6742783610)). The new AI "dream self" apps charge far more: Aya is **$9.99 a week, $29.95 a month, $149.99 a year**, plus $1.99 to unlock a single audio ([Aya IAPs](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6760195623)). Several makers charge credits for each AI creation, because AI writing and AI voices cost them money per use: ProgramU sells credit packs on top of a $19.99/month plan, JustSubly has "Whispers", Whispurry has character credits, and Sublimind allows one "Max" track a week ([ProgramU](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6767283547&country=us), [JustSubly](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6782797650&country=us)). Credits backfire when they run out quickly or vanish on failed attempts: one Whispurry reviewer spent all their starting credits on a single subliminal, and credits were taken for generations the AI refused ([Whispurry reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=6753062688/sortBy=mostRecent/json)).

The industry numbers favour a calmer version of that playbook. In Health & Fitness, **annual plans are 68% of subscriptions sold**, the median yearly price is about **$39.94**, the median trial-to-paid rate is **37.7%**, and 54% of apps use 5–9 day trials ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). A hard paywall converts **10.7% of downloads to paying users, against 2.1% for freemium** ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). Weekly plans keep only **5.5% of subscribers after a year, against 19.9% for annual** ([Adapty 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2026)). The tension is real: a hard paywall makes the most money per download, but "can't use it unless you pay" is complaint number two. The best fit for you is a **free taste with a firm gate**: ready-made tracks and one personal subliminal free, then a trial before making more, unlimited voices and offline use. Users also ask outright for a one-time purchase ("I'd buy it once for $29.99") ([Innertune reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=6443625379/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). Treat lifetime carefully, though: lifetime buyers at ThinkUp and iMobLife were locked out after changes, so offer it only if you'll honour it forever ([ThinkUp](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/id=906660772/sortBy=mostRecent/json)).

Marketing runs through social media, not ads. Paid Apple Search Ads in Health & Fitness cost roughly **$3.83 per install** ([AppTweak 2026](https://apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks)), while the median install earns only **$0.48 in its first 14 days** ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)), so paid ads lose money at launch. The winners grew through an audience: Stella's founder pointed **1.2M Instagram and 2.8M TikTok followers** at her app ([Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/blog/spite-built-this-340kmonth-app)), and self-growth app Astra got **25K downloads from a single UGC video** ([Sideshift](https://www.sideshift.app/casestudies/astra)). The subliminal creator world is reachable for a solo founder. The biggest YouTube channels are only around **200K–412K subscribers** (Eggtopia, Kapelsu, Beauty Krystalized) and are mostly faceless ([ytshark, Feb 2026](https://ytshark.com/?p=166)). Creators make modest money from Patreon tiers, sometimes under a hundred paying members ([Luminalplay Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/luminalplay)), so an affiliate code that pays them a share of each sale is an attractive offer. Store search is open too: makers that rank for "subliminal" earn under $10K a month (VibeSesh is estimated at about 60K installs) ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6746336493)), and the leaders put their keyword in the app name ("I am – Daily Affirmations", "Myla : Manifest & Vision Board") ([iTunes search](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=vision+board&entity=software&limit=25&country=us)). So your new name should carry a subtitle such as "Subliminal Maker".

Watch the rules. Apple rejects health claims it can't verify (guideline 1.4.1), can remove apps for misleading marketing "inside or outside the App Store" (2.3.1), and since November 2025 requires clear permission before personal data goes to a third-party AI such as Claude or ElevenLabs (5.1.2(i)) ([Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Google has matching rules for AI apps and user data ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17134731?hl=en)). This audience's favourite topics are appearance ("desired face", skin, hair), money and confidence ([New Statesman, Nov 2025](https://www.newstatesman.com/culture/life/2025/11/youtube-subliminals-want-to-make-you-perfect)). Your store page and any paid creator posts must not promise physical changes. Sublime's careful wording ("Subliminals do not guarantee personal change") is a good model ([Sublime site](https://www.sublimesubliminals.com/)).

## A five-step build plan to beat all of them

The order below follows one rule: **fix what makes people leave or ask for refunds before adding what makes them say "wow"**. Each step names the complaint or gap it answers. "Size" is a rough guide to effort for a Claude Code chat: small is a day or two, medium is about a week, large is several weeks.

### Step 1: Must-haves before the store release

| 2 | **Payments with RevenueCat, done honestly**: 7-day trial on annual (~$39.99), monthly (~$9.99), a reminder 2 days before billing, a cancel link in Settings, purchases that restore across iPhone, Android and web | Complaint #1; the $39.94 median annual price and 68% annual share | Medium |
| 3 | **Free taste**: all ready-made tracks plus one personal subliminal free; no forced review, no pop-up loops | Complaint #2 | Small |

| 5 | **Safe-writing checks**: no negative words in AI lines, a choice of "I am / You are / [Name] is", shuffle | Complaint #6 | Small |

### Step 5: Don't build

Skip "silent" 20 kHz encoding, flashing-text visual subliminals and booster or "remove unwanted results" tracks. Each one invites the "there's nothing in it" or "is this safe?" doubt that your transparency pitch is meant to remove, and some sellers openly say subliminals can be made to harm, which feeds that fear ([Gumroad listing](https://lushsubliminals.gumroad.com/l/ryxdbq)). Hold off on AI "manifestation songs" (Subwave) and AI chat coaches (ProgramU) until the core is loved, because they add running costs without fixing a top complaint ([Subwave](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6761197599&country=us)).

A note on effort: Step 1, item 1 (background audio on native phones) and item 2 (payments) are the riskiest technically. If the chat is on Sonnet, those two are worth doing on Opus, because a mistake there breaks overnight listening or bills people wrongly, which are exactly the complaints this plan is built to avoid.

## Conclusion

The research turns the challenge upside down. Building more features won't beat these apps: you already have more than any of them. They're losing users through broken trust, in three forms: money (surprise charges), data (lost recordings) and content (hidden lines). An app that is **visibly honest** on all three would stand alone in a crowded, young field where no maker has passed about 2,000 US ratings. The strongest story to sell is simple: "your words, your voice, nothing hidden, and it's yours to keep." That means a transparency panel, offline listening and audio export, backed by a trial reminder that actually arrives.

The open questions are commercial, not technical. Third-party revenue estimates for these apps disagree by ten times or more, so nobody outside knows how big the subliminal niche really is. And Sublime's fast releases and five-language website show that a solo rival can catch up quickly on features. Your durable advantages are running on Android and the web where Sublime doesn't, ElevenLabs voice quality, and a reputation for fair billing, which a cheap clone can't copy overnight. Pick the new name with "Subliminal Maker" in the subtitle, ship Step 1, and line up three to five mid-size faceless subliminal creators with affiliate codes before launch day.
